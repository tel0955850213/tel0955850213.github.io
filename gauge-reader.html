Industrial Gauge Reader · Engineering case study

When a gauge reader picks the wrong end

An analog gauge needle has a long, thin tip and a short, thick counterweight. During prototype testing, the camera sometimes picked up enough color on the counterweight side to report a plausible value from the wrong end of the needle.

Replay the failure

See why the counterweight can win, how the calibrated rule finds the tip, and what happens when the tip is obscured.

Reading scenario

Counterweight selected

The older rule scored each direction as a whole. A thick counterweight and nearby colored markings could outweigh the finer needle tip.

This schematic explains the decision logic using synthetic geometry.

Implementation

Find the axis, then identify the tip

  1. Align to the calibration image

    Register the camera crop to a known view of that dial. The reference provides the center, scale endpoints, and a sample of the needle color.

  2. Check both ends of the axis

    Use pixels near the center to locate the line through the pivot. Compare the two ends in a narrow band that the tip reaches and the shorter counterweight usually cannot.

  3. Confirm before displaying a value

    Convert a supported angle to a calibrated reading. Require three consistent readings for the live display. After a zoom, wait for the gauge to settle and the camera to refocus.

Verification

Trade-offs and limits

The ring check makes the answer depend on evidence from the tip itself. Its weak point is a tip that disappears under glare, blur, or low resolution. Waiting for additional readings reduces transient errors and delays the first displayed value.

Synthetic check of the angle estimator

Obscured tip
8 of 8 withheld

I checked eight dial angles twice, once with the full tip and once with the tip shortened to simulate an obstruction. The test ran the prototype’s angle estimator on 480 × 480 px synthetic images with known alignment and applied its usual frame acceptance checks. Camera motion, glare, and field accuracy remain separate measurements.

A field comparison would use the same labeled footage for both versions, hold the error tolerance fixed, and report wrong readings and withheld readings separately. Angle error belongs alongside coverage, so rejecting difficult frames cannot make the reader appear more accurate on its own.
